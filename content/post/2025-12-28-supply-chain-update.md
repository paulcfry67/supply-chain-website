### Automotive
The USA <a href="https://supplychaindaily.news/tags/automotive/">automotive</a> manufacturing industry has endured a tumultuous 2025, with tariffs, strikes, and supplier distress dominating headlines. Suppliers in North America have cut over 60,000 jobs since the year's start, as tracked by industry sources, leading to production halts and inventory shortages. For instance, U.S. auto plants are reportedly weeks away from shutdowns due to chip shortages, while foreign automakers like Subaru and Nissan have paused operations or deliveries amid trade uncertainties.

Tariffs on imported vehicles and parts have inflated costs, with heavy truck sales dropping 25.2% year-over-year by November, creating a "perfect storm" of economic pressures. This aligns with broader trade policy shifts under the Trump administration, where changes to USMCA are urged by Detroit's Big Three to prevent cheaper imports from undermining local builds. Impacts include extended delivery times—up to 30% longer for some models—and rising costs passed to consumers, potentially increasing vehicle prices by 10-15%. Short-term consequences involve factory idling and job losses, while long-term effects may spur onshoring of critical parts manufacturing.

Best practices include forging partnerships for localized sourcing and leveraging federal incentives for domestic investments, as seen in mergers and acquisitions trends this year. Companies should also monitor AI applications for forecasting disruptions, ensuring agile responses to labor unrest and policy changes.

### Construction

In the automotive context, this impacts factory expansions and EV charging infrastructure, with labor shortages compounding the problem. Recent X posts reflect sentiment of "quiet pressure" from suppliers increasing minimum order quantities (MOQs) and tightening terms in regions like Mexico and India. Short-term, businesses face halted constructions and inflated budgets; long-term, it could hinder economic recovery in manufacturing hubs.

Recommendations include adopting digital twins for supply chain simulation and securing multi-year contracts with diverse suppliers to buffer against disruptions, as demonstrated by resilient firms navigating 2025's uncertainties.
